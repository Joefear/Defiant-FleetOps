# ADR-012 — Receiving Exception Relationship Matrix, Serial-Conflict Observation Path, and Condition Semantics

## Status

Accepted

## Decision 1 — Narrow supersession of ADR-004 and ADR-010

Only the following earlier creation text is qualified for the known pre-existing SERIAL_MISMATCH observation path:

- ADR-004 Decision 9: `serialized received unit -> Asset`.
- ADR-004 Decision 11: receiving each serialized physical unit creates its Asset and initial RECEIVED transition atomically.
- ADR-010 Decision 26: the complete Asset-creation bundle for each serialized received unit.
- ADR-010 Decision 30: treatment of a readable identifier conflict already known before normal Asset creation is admitted.

The governing distinction is:

| Receiving case | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| Serialized unit admitted to normal Asset creation | Complete normal Asset bundle. |
| Readable identifier already bound to an existing canonical Asset identifier before admission | SERIAL_MISMATCH observation path; no normal Asset admission and no new Asset. |
| No known conflict at admission, followed by a concurrent canonical uniqueness failure | Complete rollback of the attempted normal bundle under ADR-010 Decision 30. |

Known pre-existing SERIAL_MISMATCH is the sole Slice 9 exception to normal serialized Asset creation. No other earlier ADR behavior is superseded. The exact textual qualification is recorded in **Superseded / qualified prior ADR text** below.

## Decision 2 — Why the qualification is necessary

The earlier ADRs established creation rules before Slice 9's complete SERIAL_MISMATCH behavior was derived. Controlled D5 already authorizes serial mismatch and requires receiving personnel to record physical reality.

A known identifier conflict cannot safely create a duplicate canonical identifier, reuse the existing Asset as the arriving unit, fabricate a different identifier, or disappear from receipt truth. An explicit observation path therefore preserves the actual arrival while withholding an unsupported claim of Asset identity.

This is a lower-tier receiving refinement for an existing exception category. It does not change D5, override identifier uniqueness, or treat a physical observation as proof that an existing Asset has returned.

## Decision 3 — Normal serialized receiving remains unchanged

A readable unique identifier follows the complete normal Asset-creation path. An unreadable identifier also follows that complete path, with the canonical unreadable representation and SERIAL_UNREADABLE. UOM mismatch, substitution, damage, opening or unexpected arrival alone does not supply another exemption from normal serialized creation.

For every unit admitted to normal creation, preserve ADR-010's entire atomic bundle:

1. Receipt/receipt-line reality, exact comparator where applicable, provenance and unit linkage.
2. The Asset against the actual serialized Item, at RECEIVED and version 1, with explicit owner, independently optional custodian, validated dock location and NULL current assignment.
3. Initial immutable lifecycle transition, NULL to RECEIVED at result version 1.
4. Exactly one ADR-007 physical-fact baseline matching the validated creation facts.
5. Exactly one ADR-008 initial-unassigned witness.
6. Required canonical identifier records.
7. All applicable independently derived receiving Exceptions.

No partial normal bundle may commit. Only the initial lifecycle transition produces global Asset version 1; witnesses add no version. Receiving retains production creation authority, with no manual Asset, baseline, witness or initial-transition endpoint.

The known-conflict branch does not waive other receiving input, credential, tenant, quantity, Item, comparator or context requirements. ADR-010's explicit serialized-unit owner input, optional custodian and governed dock requirements are preserved; none authorizes changing the existing conflicting Asset.

## Decision 4 — Complete exception vocabulary and relationship authority

The ten Slice 9 types are exactly:

`SHORT`, `OVER`, `SUBSTITUTION`, `DAMAGED`, `OPENED`, `SERIAL_UNREADABLE`, `SERIAL_MISMATCH`, `UNEXPECTED_ITEM`, `QUANTITY_VARIANCE` and `UOM_MISMATCH`.

Every type has the explicit typed shape in the normative matrix below. Generic JSON or untyped related-reference collections cannot supply relationship authority. No alias, additional category, materials exception or mutable resolution status is introduced.

## Decision 5 — Common Exception facts

Every Exception has an opaque `id`, required `org_id`, governed `exception_type`, required `receipt_id`, credential-derived `actor_id`, applicable occurred/observed time and server/database `recorded_at`.

Occurrence claims and record time remain separate UTC facts under D11. The caller cannot select authoritative organization, performing Actor or record time. Detail text cannot replace typed references or observed identifier fields.

All references must be same-tenant and context-coherent. `asset_id` always means a newly received Asset created for the referenced physical unit. `conflicting_asset_id` always means the existing canonical identifier's Asset; the two roles are not interchangeable.

## Decision 6 — SHORT

SHORT requires `receipt_id` and the exact immutable `po_line_id`. Its `receipt_line_id`, `asset_id` and `conflicting_asset_id` must all be NULL.

ADR-011's receipt-local comparison remains authoritative. When SHORT is derived, exactly one row represents that receipt/comparator disagreement; duplicates are prohibited. An included comparator with no arrivals uses the empty received set under ADR-011 without inventing a receipt line, zero-quantity physical unit or missing Asset.

## Decision 7 — OVER

OVER requires `receipt_id` and the exact immutable `po_line_id`. Its `receipt_line_id`, `asset_id` and `conflicting_asset_id` must all be NULL.

OVER describes aggregate truth for one receipt and comparator under ADR-011. When derived, exactly one row represents that disagreement. Do not select an arbitrary excess physical line or Asset as its authority.

SHORT and OVER retain their strict inequalities. A fully comparable receipt/comparator total cannot be both below and above its expectation. Neither type is forced into existence merely because its relationship shape is valid.

## Decision 8 — SUBSTITUTION

SUBSTITUTION requires `receipt_id`, `receipt_line_id` and `po_line_id`. Actual Item comes from the receipt line; expected Item comes from that exact comparator.

`asset_id` is optional only where normal serialized Asset creation succeeded for that same physical unit. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL. A substitution on a known-conflict observation remains recordable with NULL `asset_id`; any existing conflicting Asset is referenced only by the separate SERIAL_MISMATCH row.

## Decision 9 — DAMAGED

DAMAGED derives only from `receipt_line.condition = DAMAGED`. It requires `receipt_id` and `receipt_line_id`.

`po_line_id` is required and must equal the line's exact comparator when the line is PO-backed; otherwise it must be NULL. `asset_id` is optional for a normally created Asset of that unit, and NULL when none exists. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL.

Damage does not authorize a lifecycle, location, ownership or other change to an existing conflicting Asset.

## Decision 10 — OPENED

OPENED derives only from `receipt_line.condition = OPENED`. It requires `receipt_id` and `receipt_line_id`.

`po_line_id` is required and exact when the line is PO-backed and otherwise NULL. `asset_id` is optional for a normally created Asset of that unit, and NULL when none exists. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL.

OPENED records the governed receiving condition. It does not start an unobserved handling clock, introduce materials handling workflows or replace independently observed events.

## Decision 11 — Receipt condition is single-valued

Each receipt line has exactly one governed condition:

`GOOD | DAMAGED | OPENED | UNKNOWN`.

DAMAGED and OPENED are mutually exclusive derivations on one receipt line. GOOD and UNKNOWN derive neither condition Exception; UNKNOWN is not silently treated as GOOD.

Slice 9 does not add condition arrays, multiple condition rows, bit fields or a second condition column. Simultaneous opened-and-damaged representation requires separate governance if later operations need it. This restriction describes the governed single-field representation, not a claim that physical opening and physical damage cannot occur together.

Other independently true exception classes may coexist with the one condition-derived Exception.

## Decision 12 — SERIAL_UNREADABLE

SERIAL_UNREADABLE requires `receipt_id`, `receipt_line_id` and the normally created `asset_id`. `po_line_id` must equal the line's comparator when PO-backed and otherwise be NULL. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL.

The canonical identifier retains NULL `value` and a nonblank `unreadable_reason`, using ADR-010 Decision 29's existing type vocabulary. No fabricated readable serial, shared placeholder or marker text is inserted as a readable value.

The complete normal Asset bundle remains mandatory. An unreadable value does not establish a known readable-identifier conflict and cannot be routed into the no-Asset branch merely to avoid that bundle.

## Decision 13 — UNEXPECTED_ITEM

UNEXPECTED_ITEM requires `receipt_id` and `receipt_line_id`; `po_line_id` must be NULL. No fictional PO line is created.

`asset_id` is optional when normal serialized creation succeeded for the actual unit and otherwise NULL. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL. A separate SERIAL_MISMATCH may explain a known identifier conflict on the unexpected serialized observation.

The frozen accessory is nonserialized, with receipt reality and UNEXPECTED_ITEM but no Asset or inventory balance. Unexpected Items generally are not presumed nonserialized.

## Decision 14 — QUANTITY_VARIANCE

QUANTITY_VARIANCE retains the handoff meaning: a receipt line's actual quantity disagrees with the packing-reference count. It requires `receipt_id` and `receipt_line_id`.

`po_line_id` must equal the line's exact comparator when PO-backed and otherwise be NULL. Both `asset_id` and `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL, including when the line also describes a normally created serialized Asset.

This is a line-scoped packing disagreement, not a receipt/comparator aggregate or a replacement for SHORT, OVER or UOM_MISMATCH. Packing observations do not establish package-to-unit conversion, units per package or equivalence between engineering UOMs. No count is invented from the PO or from the number of Assets created.

## Decision 15 — UOM_MISMATCH

Preserve ADR-011: UOM_MISMATCH requires `receipt_id`, `receipt_line_id` and the exact immutable `po_line_id` comparator.

`asset_id` is optional for a normally created Asset of that unit and otherwise NULL. `conflicting_asset_id` must be NULL. A known serial-conflict observation with a different actual UOM may independently produce SERIAL_MISMATCH and UOM_MISMATCH.

The mismatch signals incomparability only. It establishes no equality, SHORT, OVER, substitution, packing variance or successful/failed conversion.

## Decision 16 — SERIAL_MISMATCH meaning

SERIAL_MISMATCH means a readable serialized identifier observed during receiving is already bound to an existing authoritative Asset identifier.

Match against the established tenant-scoped canonical `(org_id, type, value)` identifier uniqueness boundary. A similar string, matching value under another identifier type, another organization's identifier or caller assertion is not sufficient authority.

The match proves an identifier collision. It does not prove that the physical arriving unit is the existing Asset and authorizes no identifier duplication, Asset reuse, RMA, transfer, re-entry or automatic merge.

## Decision 17 — Check known conflicts before normal Asset admission

Before admitting normal Asset creation, FleetOps checks the observed readable identifier against canonical identifier authority in the authenticated organization.

If the identifier already belongs to an existing canonical Asset, do not attempt normal Asset creation for that unit. Select the SERIAL_MISMATCH observation path and validate the actual canonical owner used by the conflict relationship.

This is an explicit pre-admission branch, not an exception handler that recovers a failed canonical insert. Existing exact-comparator admission still applies: validate the selected immutable PO-line version and active lineage under ADR-010's retained PO serialization boundary. A known serial conflict does not admit a stale or foreign comparator.

## Decision 18 — No new Asset for a known pre-existing conflict

The known-conflict path creates no new Asset, canonical `asset_identifier`, lifecycle transition, ADR-007 baseline or ADR-008 assignment witness.

The receipt line has no received-unit Asset linkage. Do not link the existing conflicting Asset as though it were newly received, create an empty Asset awaiting repair, invent an alternative serial, or backfill an incomplete normal bundle later.

The physical unit remains an unresolved received observation. This describes its evidential status; it creates no new lifecycle state or Slice 10 resolution workflow.

## Decision 19 — Record the receipt reality

The anomaly transaction preserves the real receipt line, actual Item, captured quantity and UOM, the one governed condition, exact PO-line comparator when applicable, observed readable identifier, SERIAL_MISMATCH and its existing conflicting Asset reference, plus other independently true line Exceptions.

No Asset identity is asserted for the arriving physical unit. The receipt line and Exception still have their own opaque FleetOps identifiers.

ADR-011 compares physical receipt lines, not counts of successfully created Assets. A comparable known-conflict line therefore remains part of its receipt/comparator population; an incompatible-UOM line still blocks a complete local SHORT/OVER/equality conclusion. Absence of a new Asset does not remove the arrival from receiving quantity truth.

## Decision 20 — Explicit observation fields and unit granularity

Use explicit immutable fields on the receipt line for this anomaly observation:

- `observed_identifier_type`
- `observed_identifier_value`

The type uses the existing vocabulary: `MANUFACTURER_SERIAL`, `PCB_SERIAL`, `MAC`, `IMEI` or `OTHER`. The value is the actual readable observation, satisfying the existing readable-value contract. Matching uses the existing canonical type/value equality; no new normalization, conversion or identity rule is introduced.

These fields hold the known-conflict observation. Both are required on that path and both are NULL on other receiving lines, whose normal canonical readable/unreadable identifier facts continue under ADR-010 Decision 29. They are not `asset_identifiers`, canonical identity, FleetOps keys or authority to alter an existing Asset. Their values need not be globally unique: independent physical observations of the same conflicting serial must remain recordable.

A serialized receiving receipt line represents exactly one observed physical unit. Multiple physical serialized units require distinct receipt lines, even when they share the same Item and comparator. One type/value pair must never stand for several units or an undifferentiated quantity batch. Nonserialized quantity lines remain permitted.

This selects unit granularity within the handoff's existing receipt-line model, which imposes no requirement to combine several serialized units into one line. No receiving schema exists at this checkpoint that prevents it. One unit per serialized line also makes the normal received-Asset relationship and this observation relationship unambiguous.

Preserve the quantity and UOM actually captured for that unit. Unit granularity does not authorize changing a UOM to EA, deriving physical unit count through conversion, or inventing a quantity allocation from an aggregate observation. If future implementation cannot faithfully represent one physical serialized observation with this granularity, STOP rather than silently group units, fabricate per-unit facts, use generic JSON or insert a canonical identifier to retain the anomaly.

## Decision 21 — Distinct conflicting Asset relationship

Use the typed Exception reference `conflicting_asset_id`.

For SERIAL_MISMATCH, `receipt_id`, `receipt_line_id` and `conflicting_asset_id` are required. The referenced receipt line must contain the required immutable observed identifier type and value from Decision 20. They are not duplicated as a second authoritative pair on the Exception.

`po_line_id` must equal that line's comparator when PO-backed and otherwise be NULL. `asset_id` must be NULL.

The conflicting reference must identify the same-organization Asset whose canonical identifier owns that exact observed type/value. An arbitrary existing Asset, even in the same organization, cannot satisfy this relationship. Never overload `asset_id` with conflicting-Asset semantics.

## Decision 22 — Existing Asset is context only

The existing conflicting Asset and its canonical identifier remain unchanged by receiving the anomaly. Do not change lifecycle, owner, custody, location, assignment, configuration, identifier rows or projections.

The reference explains the collision without asserting movement, return, custody or new ownership of that existing Asset. Preserving the observed serial is not permission to relabel, merge or repair either physical unit.

## Decision 23 — Concurrent uniqueness race still rolls back normally

If pre-admission lookup finds no conflict but a concurrent transaction claims the identifier before canonical insertion, the existing uniqueness constraint rejects the attempted normal creation.

ADR-010 Decision 30 remains fully governing for this case. Roll back the entire attempted normal bundle, including its receipt-line/unit linkage and Exceptions; no orphan receiving state from that failed attempt may remain.

Do not catch the failed insert and convert that same transaction, or a savepoint within it, into a committed SERIAL_MISMATCH observation. A retry after the competing transaction commits is a fresh admission attempt. It can now discover the existing canonical conflict and select the observation path cleanly.

## Decision 24 — Known conflict and race have different authority

A known conflict supplies authoritative canonical evidence before normal Asset admission; that evidence is validated as part of the anomaly transaction.

A race invalidates the attempted normal transaction's admission assumptions. Its outcome is rollback, not error-dependent fallback writes. This distinction preserves both the normal bundle's atomicity and the anomaly path's deliberate admission.

A lookup does not replace canonical uniqueness enforcement. PostgreSQL remains the final guard against competing normal admissions.

## Decision 25 — SERIAL_MISMATCH observation atomicity

The known-conflict transaction atomically commits receipt-line reality, the immutable observed identifier fields, the conflicting-Asset relationship, SERIAL_MISMATCH and every independently required line Exception. Any receipt header or other receiving records newly created in that transaction share its rollback.

Failure rolls back all anomaly-path records in the transaction together. A line cannot commit with only its observed serial and no required conflict Exception; an Exception cannot commit without its receipt reality and canonical-owner evidence.

No incomplete Asset bundle commits because normal Asset creation was never admitted. This is not permission to weaken the complete bundle on normal readable, unreadable or other non-conflict receiving paths.

## Decision 26 — Condition plus SERIAL_MISMATCH

A known-conflict receipt line retains exactly one condition:

- DAMAGED derives SERIAL_MISMATCH plus DAMAGED.
- OPENED derives SERIAL_MISMATCH plus OPENED.
- GOOD or UNKNOWN derives no additional condition Exception.

Neither case permits DAMAGED plus OPENED on the same line. The condition Exception has NULL `asset_id` because no new Asset exists, and NULL `conflicting_asset_id` because only SERIAL_MISMATCH carries that relationship.

## Decision 27 — Multiple independent Exceptions

Examples of valid combinations, when independently supported, are:

- SUBSTITUTION plus DAMAGED.
- SUBSTITUTION plus UOM_MISMATCH.
- SERIAL_MISMATCH plus DAMAGED.
- SERIAL_MISMATCH plus OPENED.
- SERIAL_MISMATCH plus UOM_MISMATCH.

Distinct source facts may produce distinct types; one fact must not be counted repeatedly or repackaged as another classification. For these line-scoped Slice 9 observations, one line/type disagreement produces one Exception. SHORT and OVER use receipt/comparator scope instead. Reconciliation must prevent duplicate same-type rows for the same disagreement instance.

DAMAGED plus OPENED cannot derive from one single-valued condition. Sharing a receipt does not collapse genuinely separate lines' observations.

## Decision 28 — Durable context coherence

Durably enforce all of the following:

- Every Exception belongs to its authenticated organization and one receipt.
- A referenced receipt line belongs to that receipt and organization.
- A PO-backed line's Exception references the same exact PO-line version as the line; that version belongs to the receipt's PO and organization.
- For a line without a comparator, conditional Exception comparator references are NULL, even if other lines in the same receipt are PO-backed.
- SHORT and OVER reference an exact comparator in the receipt's PO without an arbitrary physical-line or Asset reference.
- An optional `asset_id` identifies the newly created Asset for that exact receiving unit, never another same-tenant Asset.
- `conflicting_asset_id` is allowed only for SERIAL_MISMATCH and identifies the actual canonical owner of the line's observed type/value.
- SERIAL_MISMATCH has no received-unit Asset linkage; its conflict reference cannot occupy both semantic roles.

Tenant-safe foreign keys alone do not validate the entire graph. Required condition, comparator, observation and canonical-owner relationships must also hold at the durable boundary. No arbitrary same-organization graph stitching is permitted.

## Decision 29 — PostgreSQL enforces type shapes

PostgreSQL must enforce the matrix's required references, required NULLs, conditional PO-backed references and conflict-only relationship. It must also enforce the single condition value, condition-derived Exception coherence, known-conflict observation requirements and context relationships.

Use the established tenant-safe constraint and narrowly governed write-boundary patterns under ADR-001, ADR-002 and ADR-006. Pydantic or service validation alone is insufficient. The exact migration, constraint and atomic-boundary implementation belongs to a separately opened Slice 9.

Keep canonical readable identifier uniqueness intact: the existing unique `(org_id, type, value)` boundary for non-NULL values remains authoritative. Observation fields must not be inserted into that canonical namespace.

## Decision 30 — Server derives classification; no generic Exception API

There is no generic `POST /exceptions` or equivalent caller-selected classification operation.

The server derives SHORT, OVER, SUBSTITUTION, SERIAL_MISMATCH, QUANTITY_VARIANCE, UOM_MISMATCH and UNEXPECTED_ITEM from validated receipt, expectation, packing and canonical identifier facts. The single observed condition derives DAMAGED or OPENED. The governed unreadable identifier representation derives SERIAL_UNREADABLE.

The caller supplies observed facts and legitimate business targets, not authority to select or suppress Exception types, choose an arbitrary conflicting Asset, or alter comparison rules.

## Decision 31 — Immutable Exceptions and observations

All Slice 9 Exceptions are immutable. Ordinary runtime authority has no UPDATE, DELETE or TRUNCATE path for them.

Receipt reality, including the known-conflict observed identifier fields, remains immutable. A later receipt, successful retry elsewhere or operator discovery cannot rewrite an earlier observation, retarget its comparator, replace its conflicting Asset or delete its Exceptions.

D6's reversal-plus-correct-entry doctrine remains governing. Corrections and Exception resolution belong to Slice 10; no mutable status, repair endpoint or overwrite workflow is introduced here.

## Decision 32 — Tenancy and authenticated Actor

All receiving, Exception, observation and conflicting-Asset relationships are tenant-safe. ADR-002 fail-closed RLS and explicit trusted-organization checks at elevated write boundaries remain mandatory.

ADR-006's verified credential authority supplies the performing Actor, including at the durable write boundary. Caller-supplied Actor, another active same-organization Actor or organization context alone cannot authorize receiving attribution. A conflict in another tenant must neither produce a same-tenant SERIAL_MISMATCH nor expose that tenant's identifier owner.

## Decision 33 — Architecture status

No Architecture Amendment Request is required for this decision. The governing DOCX and amended Markdown were checked; no controlled rule requires every serialized physical observation, including a known serial-conflict anomaly, to become an Asset.

D5 already names serial mismatch and requires truthful physical receiving records. D1 separates permanent opaque identities from serials; an opaque receipt-line/Exception identity need not assert Asset identity. D3's history/projection rules still govern every Asset actually created. The known-conflict path creates neither an Asset projection nor a fictitious Asset transition.

Section 9 requires nine Asset identities for its particular delivered serialized units. That fixture contains an unreadable serial and substitution, but no known serial conflict, so all nine still follow normal creation. Section 6's physical-truth ownership and Sections 8 and 10's build boundaries remain intact; no RMA, transfer, return, merge or materials workflow is introduced.

If implementation reveals a controlled Sections 5–10 requirement incompatible with this path, STOP for an Architecture Amendment Request. A schema shortcut or this ADR's lower-tier status cannot amend controlled architecture.

## Normative complete relationship matrix

Required means NOT NULL. NULL means prohibited. Conditional PO backing is determined by the referenced receipt line: if it has a comparator, the Exception must reference that exact UUID; if it has none, the Exception comparator must be NULL. The existence of a PO on the receipt header alone does not make an unexpected line PO-backed.

Optional `asset_id` is permitted only for the normally created Asset of that physical unit. It is NULL for known-conflict observations and nonserialized reality.

| Type | receipt_id | receipt_line_id | po_line_id | asset_id | conflicting_asset_id |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SHORT | Required | NULL | Required | NULL | NULL |
| OVER | Required | NULL | Required | NULL | NULL |
| SUBSTITUTION | Required | Required | Required | Optional | NULL |
| DAMAGED | Required | Required | PO-backed: required; otherwise NULL | Optional | NULL |
| OPENED | Required | Required | PO-backed: required; otherwise NULL | Optional | NULL |
| SERIAL_UNREADABLE | Required | Required | PO-backed: required; otherwise NULL | Required | NULL |
| SERIAL_MISMATCH | Required | Required | PO-backed: required; otherwise NULL | NULL | Required |
| UNEXPECTED_ITEM | Required | Required | NULL | Optional | NULL |
| QUANTITY_VARIANCE | Required | Required | PO-backed: required; otherwise NULL | NULL | NULL |
| UOM_MISMATCH | Required | Required | Required | Optional | NULL |

SERIAL_MISMATCH additionally requires the referenced receipt line's immutable `observed_identifier_type` and `observed_identifier_value`, matching the canonical identifier owned by `conflicting_asset_id`. Those fields live on the receipt line, not in generic JSON or a duplicate canonical identifier row.

## Superseded / qualified prior ADR text

**ADR-004 Decision 9**

`serialized received unit -> Asset`

is qualified to:

`serialized received unit admitted to normal Asset creation -> Asset.`

The underlying eligibility rule remains unchanged: every Asset must reference an actual serialized Item; nonserialized receipt lines still create no Asset or inventory balance.

**ADR-004 Decision 11**

"receiving each serialized physical unit creates its Asset and initial RECEIVED transition atomically"

is qualified to:

"receiving each serialized physical unit admitted to normal Asset creation creates its Asset and initial RECEIVED transition atomically."

Known pre-existing SERIAL_MISMATCH is not admitted to normal Asset creation. Receiving retains exclusive production Asset-creation authority; the qualification supplies no manual onboarding shortcut.

**ADR-010 Decision 26**

"For each serialized received unit"

is qualified to:

"For each serialized received unit admitted to normal Asset creation."

Its entire coherent creation bundle remains mandatory for every normally admitted unit. The known pre-existing SERIAL_MISMATCH observation is the sole Slice 9 exception.

**ADR-010 Decision 30**

A known pre-existing conflict, detected before admission, follows the governed SERIAL_MISMATCH observation transaction. It is not a failed canonical insert.

A concurrent uniqueness race after normal admission remains subject to complete atomic rollback. The failed normal transaction cannot be converted in place into an observation transaction. A fresh retry may then discover the pre-existing conflict.

No other ADR-004 or ADR-010 text is superseded. References elsewhere to their creation requirements, including the handoff's serialized-unit creation sentence, apply with these expressly recorded qualifications; they do not restore universal creation for the sole known-conflict case. All other normal creation, invariant, uniqueness and workflow requirements remain unchanged.

ADR-011's population remains all receipt lines in the authenticated organization with the same receipt UUID and exact PO-line UUID. Earlier/later receipts and other comparators are excluded. Any incomparable UOM within that population blocks the complete local SHORT/OVER/equality conclusion, including when a comparable subtotal alone reaches or exceeds expectation. Linked substitution and known-conflict physical quantities are not omitted merely because of their Exception types.

Full-delivery and line-by-line capture must continue to satisfy the frozen exact-four result without duplicate or disposable intermediate Exceptions. API/transaction mechanics remain Slice 9 implementation work; they may not change the comparison population, mutate committed history or weaken cardinality.

The architecture requires nine Assets in the frozen scenario, not an unqualified Asset for every possible anomalous observation. The broader universal creation wording was in the earlier ADRs and handoff and is now explicitly bounded. This conclusion is a design compatibility finding, not a claim that production behavior has been implemented or tested.
