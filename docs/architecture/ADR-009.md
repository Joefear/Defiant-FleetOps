# ADR-009 — Immutable PO-Line Supersession and Active-Line Semantics

## Status

Accepted

## Context and authority

Slice 8 stopped before schema work at checkpoint `7ccf400bcd413fb5bab74f81b7232bad1712caa8`. [Build Handoff v1.3](FleetOps_v0.1_Build_Handoff_v1.3.docx), Slice 8, requires that issued lines be immutable, that an amendment create a new line with `supersedes_line_id`, and that the old line be "marked superseded." Its acceptance requires both lines to remain in history with only the new one active. The listed schema contains neither a superseded marker nor an explicit definition of derived active status.

That wording does not settle whether marking the old line requires an UPDATE or is a conclusion drawn from its successor. Frozen D5 preserves original expectation separately from receipt reality; D6 preserves correction history without rewriting originals. Neither supplies this missing procurement representation. ADR-008's derived assignment intervals are explicitly limited to assignment and do not independently authorize a PO-line representation.

Authority for this design gate is:

1. Frozen FleetOps Architecture & Boundary document.
2. Accepted ADR-001 through ADR-008.
3. Current FleetOps Build Handoff v1.3.
4. Candidate ADR-009 under review.

The frozen [source DOCX](FleetOps_Architecture_Boundary_v0.1.docx), with its [working Markdown transcription](FleetOps_Architecture_Boundary_v0.1.md), governs. [ADR-001](ADR-001.md) retains layer and privilege-boundary responsibilities; [ADR-002](ADR-002.md) retains tenancy; [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) retains catalog and external-reference semantics; [ADR-006](ADR-006.md) retains credential-derived Actor attribution. ADR-004/005 and ADR-007/008 retain their Asset initialization, version, history, and creation-witness contracts.

This ADR resolves the bounded Slice 8 representation within the frozen architecture. It requires no Architecture Amendment Request. Independent review and a separately opened Slice 8 implementation remain required; this document-only gate creates no procurement capability and does not itself reopen implementation.

## Decision 1 — Issued expectation is permanently immutable

Once a line belongs to an ISSUED PO, its recorded expectation is immutable. Frozen fields include at minimum `item_id`, `quantity`, `uom`, `unit_price`, `expected_date`, `line_number`, and `supersedes_line_id`, plus any other field that forms the issued expectation. Permanent UUID identity, tenant/PO relationships, and original attribution must also remain protected; moving a row to another PO cannot bypass the freeze.

The freeze survives any later permitted PO status change. CLOSED or CANCELLED must not make previously issued lines editable. Issued history must not be deleted to remove an expectation or reactivate an earlier version. Enforcement belongs at the PostgreSQL boundary as well as in Python. Under the handoff's acceptance, ordinary migrator UPDATE of frozen fields must fail while the trigger is active; deliberately disabling enforcement is a separate privileged action, not the acceptance proof.

No mutable issued-line status flag is introduced to mark supersession.

## Decision 2 — Only the new line records supersession

An amendment creates exactly one new immutable line with its own FleetOps UUIDv7 and `supersedes_line_id` equal to the predecessor's UUID. The predecessor is not updated or deleted. Its entire recorded row, including expectation and attribution, remains unchanged by the amendment.

The new line's backward reference is the authoritative supersession fact. Do not store `is_superseded`, `active`, `inactive`, `superseded_at`, or `superseded_by_id` on the predecessor as fields requiring later mutation. No separate predecessor-maintenance operation is needed.

## Decision 3 — Active and superseded are derived

A line is ACTIVE if and only if no valid successor in the same organization and PO supersedes it. A line is SUPERSEDED when another valid line in that PO directly or transitively succeeds it through the chain. These are query/read-model conclusions, never mutable stored authority.

"Later" means successor in the pointer graph, not a greater timestamp, UUID, or incidental insertion order. Validity is enforced when admitting the successor; a reader must not silently ignore a stored amendment based on its business payload and thereby reactivate an earlier line.

## Decision 4 — Each lineage has one active leaf

Every existing logical procurement lineage is a finite, non-branching, acyclic chain with one root and exactly one active leaf. A root has `supersedes_line_id = NULL`. A lineage containing only that root has the root as its active leaf.

For example, L2 points backward to L1 and L3 points backward to L2. L1 and L2 are derived superseded; L3 is derived active. All three immutable expectations remain reconstructable. An amendment changes which leaf is active without modifying any prior record.

## Decision 5 — Supersession stays within one PO

Successor and predecessor must share the same `org_id` and `po_id`. A valid UUID in another PO is not a valid predecessor, even within the same organization. A cross-organization predecessor is likewise invalid.

Use durable tenant-safe relationships under ADR-002, including composite foreign keys where applicable. Service validation alone does not establish this invariant.

## Decision 6 — The pointer establishes logical succession

Supersession represents an amendment to an existing procurement expectation. The successor may carry different Item, quantity, unit price, or expected date where Slice 8 authorizes those business inputs. Its own UOM follows the creation rule in Decision 22 and may therefore differ from the predecessor's snapshot.

Do not require identical Item or quantity across versions. Changing what is expected from a supplier can be a legitimate procurement amendment; the backward pointer preserves what the earlier expectation was. This does not authorize changing a PO to disguise what actually arrived.

## Decision 7 — Versions retain the same business line number

All records in one lineage share the same PO and business `line_number`. v1.3 does not require a new business line number for an amendment, so this ADR adopts:

```text
same organization and PO
same logical line_number
new opaque line UUID
supersedes_line_id = prior line UUID
```

For example, PO 123 / line 4 can have several immutable versions, each with a distinct UUID. `line_number` groups the business line; the UUID identifies the particular record. No line-number tuple becomes a replacement primary identity or a sufficient reference to a particular expectation version.

## Decision 8 — One lineage per logical line number

For every represented `(org_id, po_id, line_number)`, there must be exactly one lineage and one active leaf at every committed state. Independent roots with the same logical line number are invalid: preventing branches alone would not prevent that duplicate-active-line case.

Historical versions in that lineage may share `line_number`. Therefore a simple `UNIQUE (org_id, po_id, line_number)` across every version is prohibited. Slice 8 must durably enforce unique roots, same-line-number succession, and non-branching chains without excluding historical versions. Concurrent creation of independent roots for the same logical line must not evade that rule. Exact database mechanisms belong to implementation and its review.

## Decision 9 — A predecessor has at most one successor

A prior line may be directly superseded by at most one line. L2 and L3 cannot both point to L1. Conceptually enforce uniqueness equivalent to:

```text
UNIQUE (org_id, supersedes_line_id)
WHERE supersedes_line_id IS NOT NULL
```

A stronger equivalent may include the same-PO relationship. NULL predecessors permit separate logical root lines, subject to Decision 8. This durable non-branching rule must hold for direct permitted SQL and competing transactions, not merely after a service lookup.

## Decision 10 — Cycles cannot be admitted

Reject self-reference, two-node cycles, and longer cycles. Prefer an append structure in which each new line can point only to an already-established predecessor and no existing pointer can change. Adding a new node with an edge only into an existing acyclic chain preserves acyclicity.

The implementation must prove the "already-established" premise at the database boundary. An ordinary foreign key alone is not proof that a predecessor existed before the inserting statement: multi-row or deferred-reference inserts must not admit mutually referencing new rows. Cover those cases and relevant concurrent insertion paths. Neither UUIDv7 ordering nor timestamps prove backward lineage.

If durable insertion rules make cycles structurally impossible, document that proof and avoid an unnecessary recursive trigger. Otherwise use durable rejection whose concurrency behavior is proved. An unresolved safe-enforcement design requires STOP, not a weak self-cycle-only check.

## Decision 11 — The predecessor pointer never changes

`supersedes_line_id` is immutable from creation, including on a DRAFT line and when its original value is NULL. It cannot later be attached, detached, or redirected. A line cannot be moved into another lineage through pointer, tenant, PO, or logical-line changes.

A mistaken historical amendment is not corrected by pointer UPDATE. True record correction remains a future D6 workflow preserving originals, as distinguished in Decision 26.

## Decision 12 — An ISSUED PO accepts immutable amendments

An ISSUED PO may receive a successor to its current active line through the bounded amendment operation. The successor is an issued expectation from creation, with its immutable fields frozen immediately.

The PO is not reopened, and existing lines never become temporarily editable. This authority is to append a successor, not to replace an old row or invent an unrestricted post-issue line-authoring workflow.

## Decision 13 — Draft authoring remains mutable within its limits

While the PO is DRAFT, its current draft lines may receive the business edits authorized by Slice 8. There is no requirement to preserve every pre-issue keystroke as immutable history. Draft editability does not authorize changing permanent identity, tenant authority, creator attribution, or the immutable predecessor pointer.

The handoff independently makes `uom` immutable from line creation. Draft editing must retain that snapshot and must not refresh it from a changed Item default. This ADR supplies no caller UOM override. Other exact draft field permissions remain Slice 8 work under the governing handoff.

After issuance, expectation changes require an immutable successor. Mutable authoring and issued history retain their distinct meanings.

## Decision 14 — Issuance and freeze are atomic

Issuance freezes the current active draft line set atomically with `status = ISSUED` and the governing server-owned issuance time and Actor attribution. No committed state may show ISSUED while those expectations remain normally mutable.

Concurrent draft authoring and issuance must share an effective database serialization boundary. A draft mutation either belongs to the expectation frozen by issuance or rejects after the freeze; a stale service check cannot authorize a later write. The exact locking/enforcement mechanism belongs to Slice 8, without a post-commit freeze step or a PO-wide version subsystem.

## Decision 15 — Amendment is one atomic append

Within one transaction the operation must:

1. Validate tenant scope and credential-derived Actor authority.
2. Validate that the PO is ISSUED and permits amendment.
3. Identify the requested predecessor by its tenant-scoped UUID.
4. Verify that it remains the active leaf of the same PO and logical line.
5. Append exactly one valid successor with authenticated creation attribution and its own expectation snapshots.
6. Preserve the predecessor unchanged and commit all effects together.

Invalid state, reference, credential, or stale predecessor rejects without a successor or partial domain writes. There is no second transaction or later predecessor UPDATE. Database admission must remain valid through transaction completion, including concurrent status or lineage operations where those operations are authorized.

## Decision 16 — Competing amendments have one winner

Two otherwise valid concurrent attempts to supersede the same active leaf must produce exactly one accepted successor. The loser either observes that its requested predecessor is no longer active after locking or fails a durable non-branching constraint. It writes no additional successor, and the final lineage has one active leaf.

Use row locking, durable unique enforcement, or both as required to satisfy the complete contract. Do not automatically retarget the losing request to the winner's new leaf; that would authorize an amendment of an expectation the request did not select. Tests must exercise independent real PostgreSQL transactions, not only sequential duplicate requests.

## Decision 17 — No procurement version system

Do not add `po.version`, `purchase_order_version`, a procurement event register, a global PO event sequence, or an equivalent optimistic-concurrency subsystem solely to implement supersession. The requested predecessor UUID, lineage invariants, and necessary transactional enforcement supply this boundary.

Procurement writes neither increment `assets.version` nor create Asset version-producing history. ADR-005's global Asset protocol remains unchanged and does not become a PO protocol.

## Decision 18 — The active query uses successor absence

For each tenant-visible PO line, derive active status conceptually as:

```text
NOT EXISTS (
    successor in the same organization and PO
    WHERE successor.supersedes_line_id = line.id
)
```

Together with one root per logical line, same-line-number edges, non-branching, and acyclicity, this yields exactly one active leaf per lineage. Every non-leaf has a direct successor, so transitive history does not require a separate mutable status or a recursive active-status update.

A bounded read model may expose `active` and `superseded` booleans derived from these relationships. Queries operate within the transaction's visible committed history and tenant scope; they cannot independently choose another active version based on timestamp or payload.

## Decision 19 — Pointers determine history order

Reconstruct a lineage from the active leaf backward through `supersedes_line_id`, or from its root forward through the unique successor relation. Root-to-leaf order follows the graph alone. Timestamps, UUID ordering, and insertion order are not lineage authority.

Retain the applicable occurrence and server-record times as separate UTC facts under D11 and the handoff. Conflicting or equal time claims do not reorder the chain or justify rewriting a prior timestamp. Exact record fields remain Slice 8 schema work; this ordering decision creates no extra event clock or sequence.

## Decision 20 — Original expectation remains visible

A reader must be able to obtain the original issued expectation, every amendment, and the current active expectation without depending on mutable predecessor fields. An active-only convenience read must not replace the history surface or hide the superseded versions from reconstruction.

Slice 9 may compare against an active expectation under its receiving contract while retaining all prior expectations. Neither receiving nor reconciliation may amend or overwrite a PO merely to make it agree with physical arrival. Procurement amendments do not erase the fact that an earlier expectation differed.

## Decision 21 — Receiving references an exact version

Future receiving must be able to reference the opaque UUID of the specific PO-line version used as its expected comparator. A PO number, line number, or current-active lookup alone cannot preserve that reference: the active leaf may change after receiving.

Receipt history retains the selected version rather than dynamically retargeting to a later amendment. This preserves which expectation was referenced at receiving; a timestamp alone does not prove historical active status. Exact receiving schema and comparator selection belong to Slice 9. Neither this ADR nor Slice 8 implements receipts or creates Assets.

## Decision 22 — Every version has its own UOM snapshot

v1.3 requires `uom` to be copied from the Item at line creation and remain immutable thereafter. Each successor therefore copies the current default UOM of its own selected Item when the amendment is created. It does not inherit a historical UOM merely because it follows the same business line number, and the caller cannot supply an override.

The existing catalog field is `items.uom`; it is the Item default, not a historical-line lookup. Later Item updates alter no existing root or successor snapshot. Use the existing governed UOM vocabulary; this ADR does not add packaging units or conversion rules.

## Decision 23 — Price belongs to each expectation version

Every version retains its own `unit_price`. Changing an issued price appends a successor with the amended price; it never rewrites the predecessor's price. Permitted draft authoring remains governed by Decision 13.

Unit price is procurement context only. This decision creates no accounting calculation, currency field or subsystem, tax, cost allocation, payment, or invoice workflow. Numeric validation and precision remain Slice 8 implementation details subject to the handoff.

## Decision 24 — Attribution follows ADR-006

PO creation, permitted draft edits, issuance, and supersession use the existing credential-derived Actor boundary. Every successor records creation attribution sufficient to identify who recorded the amendment. Original creator attribution remains protected.

The database must derive or enforce the performer through `current_authenticated_actor()` and the accepted credential mechanism. Request Actor fields, SQL Actor testimony, arbitrary Actor GUCs, and organization-only context cannot select another performer. Mutable attribution-bearing updates record the authenticated updater and database/server update time together, including permitted direct `fleetops_app` SQL. Server-owned creation, update, issuance, and record times cannot become caller authority.

Preserve the three ADR-006 execution domains and their credential restrictions. Prefer ordinary RLS-protected writes and narrow invariant enforcement under ADR-001; this ADR grants no blanket authorization for new Category-2 SECURITY DEFINER procedures. Any privileged boundary must independently satisfy ADR-001/002/006.

## Decision 25 — Amendment authority is limited to ISSUED

For Slice 8, supersession is permitted only while the PO is ISSUED. DRAFT uses its authorized authoring operations. CLOSED and CANCELLED do not gain amendment authority merely because they appear in the status set.

This ADR does not define complete close/cancel workflows or authorize additional transition endpoints. Any later authorized status transition must preserve the permanent issued-history freeze. If Slice 8 needs unresolved status semantics beyond this scope, STOP for a separate decision.

## Decision 26 — Amendment is distinct from record correction

"We changed what we expect from the supplier" is a procurement amendment. "We entered the historical record incorrectly" is a record correction. Supersession records the former and does not serve as a shortcut around D6 for the latter.

True corrections remain Slice 10: reversing and correct entries, references to what they correct, a stated reason, and preservation of originals under D6. This ADR neither designs that workflow nor changes the correction doctrine. A mistaken historical amendment cannot be fixed by editing its original values or pointer.

## Decision 27 — Tenancy protects the whole lineage

Every successor/predecessor relationship requires the same `org_id`, `po_id`, and logical `line_number`. PO, Item, and Actor relationships remain tenant-safe under ADR-002. Vendor remains a Party with the governing vendor role and never becomes the Item manufacturer merely by supplying it, preserving ADR-003 and D7.

Tenant-owned procurement records require non-null organization scope, RLS with USING and WITH CHECK, fail-closed missing-context access, tenant-safe references, and no runtime TRUNCATE. Any elevated operation must explicitly check organization and target relationships rather than rely on owner RLS. Isolation proofs execute as the actual runtime role on PostgreSQL 16.

## Decision 28 — Business and external identifiers remain references

Every immutable line version retains permanent opaque FleetOps identity. `line_number` groups a logical line; a vendor line code, ERP line ID, or chain label does not become FleetOps primary identity. A reference to one issued version uses its UUID.

External identifiers remain references under D2 and ADR-003, with their existing attachment/search semantics. This ADR does not create an external identifier uniqueness assumption, upstream procurement authority, or a new chain-identity subsystem.

## Bounded handoff refinement

The v1.3 Slice 8 phrase "marks the old line superseded" means that the NEW immutable successor points to the predecessor through `supersedes_line_id`; the predecessor's superseded/active status is derived from that relationship. It does not authorize UPDATE of an issued predecessor.

"Only the new one is active" means that the leaf at the end of the non-branching supersession chain is the active expectation. Active status is derived, not mutable stored state. Later amendments advance the leaf again while preserving every earlier record.

This ADR makes the necessary associated rules explicit: permanent issued expectation freeze, immutable backward pointers, same-PO/same-line-number lineage, one root and active leaf per logical line, no branches or cycles, atomic issuance/amendment, and one winner for competing amendments. v1.3's named quantity/UOM/price trigger protection is a minimum, not permission to mutate other issued expectation fields.

All other Slice 8 requirements remain governing, including UOM immutability from creation, authenticated attribution, procurement-only scope, and the handoff's active-trigger migrator proof. No v1.4 is required solely because this ADR resolves the representation. The frozen architecture, ADR-001 through ADR-008, and source handoff remain unchanged.

## Required future Slice 8 test implications

These are future implementation obligations, not tests created or claimed to pass by this document-only gate. Slice 8 must prove at minimum:

1. Every issued expectation field in Decision 1 rejects UPDATE at PostgreSQL, including ordinary migrator UPDATE with the trigger active; failed writes leave values unchanged.
2. Superseding a line preserves the entire predecessor row byte/value unchanged, including attribution and timestamps.
3. Successor insertion makes the predecessor derived superseded without predecessor UPDATE.
4. The new successor is derived active.
5. Both original and successor remain available in history.
6. Valid same-PO succession succeeds with durable relationship enforcement.
7. Cross-organization supersession rejects.
8. Same-organization cross-PO supersession rejects.
9. Self-supersession rejects.
10. A second direct successor to the same predecessor rejects without creating a branch.
11. Two-node and longer cycles cannot be created, including multi-row/deferred-reference attempts and relevant concurrency paths under the chosen structural enforcement.
12. `supersedes_line_id` cannot be changed after creation, including NULL-to-reference, detachment, redirection, and draft-row attempts.
13. Every successor retains its lineage's logical `line_number`; a mismatched line number rejects.
14. Multiple historical versions legitimately share one logical line number; independent duplicate roots reject, including competing root creation.
15. Two concurrent supersessions of one active leaf yield exactly one winner, no losing successor or partial domain writes, and one final active leaf.
16. The active query returns exactly one leaf for every lineage, including an unamended root and a chain of at least three versions.
17. Authorized draft edits remain possible before issuance, with correct database-bound updater attribution and time; UOM and pointer immutability remain enforced.
18. Issuance freezes the active draft set atomically, including concurrent draft editing/creation at the issuance boundary.
19. An amendment on an ISSUED PO creates an immediately frozen successor without reopening the PO or making older lines editable.
20. Later Item default changes leave every prior UOM snapshot unchanged.
21. A successor captures its selected Item's current default UOM as its own snapshot, including a changed Item/default; caller UOM override is rejected.
22. Amending price leaves the predecessor's `unit_price` unchanged.
23. The successor records the amended price independently.
24. Each historical version remains addressable by its own durable opaque UUID for future receiving references; line number alone cannot select a version.
25. Credential-derived Actor attribution cannot be spoofed through HTTP inputs, direct runtime SQL, arbitrary Actor GUCs, or organization-only context; original attribution remains protected.
26. No PO/global procurement version system or register is introduced, and procurement changes do not affect global Asset versions or Asset history.
27. CLOSED/CANCELLED reject amendment; any authorized later status path cannot thaw previously issued history or permit its deletion.
28. Supersession creates neither receipt nor Asset records, and no receiving/Asset-creation route is added.
29. No mutable active/superseded authority column is required or introduced; issued-row deletion cannot reactivate a predecessor.
30. Root-to-leaf reconstruction follows pointers deterministically despite equal/reversed timestamps or UUID order, preserving all original expectations.

Database, tenancy, immutability, and concurrency proofs use real PostgreSQL 16. Keep all other opened Slice 8 requirements, including vendor/Item tenant safety, strict bounded request models, migration round trip, and prior invariant regressions. RLS/privilege assertions run as the role whose boundary is being tested; privileged setup is not runtime proof. Do not weaken prior assertions to accommodate procurement.
